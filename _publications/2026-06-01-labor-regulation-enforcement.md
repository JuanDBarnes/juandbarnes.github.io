Peru had one of the highest rates of labor informality in Latin America. From 2014, a national agency,
SUNAFIL, replaced the regional labor inspectorates department by department. We study its effects on
large formal firms. Using a firm panel from Peru's Annual Economic Survey and the staggered rollout, we
estimate imputation-based difference-in-differences with wild-bootstrap inference. After SUNAFIL
arrives, the share of firms paying dismissal compensation rises by about 4 percentage points (2013
share: 28%). Treated firms paid less than predicted just before arrival, so we interpret the sign of
this effect, not its size; for all firms, the sign holds across specifications and samples. Labor cost
per worker is unchanged and employment does not fall. Sales rise modestly and productivity shows
suggestive gains, which, though not causal, suggest that stricter enforcement need not hurt firm
performance and may even improve it. We cannot separate better compliance from more compensated
dismissals.
